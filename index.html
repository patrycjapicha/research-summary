✳ CUSTOMER RESEARCH · OCTOBER 2026

From insight
to action.

What happens after a dashboard raises a question? A research synthesis on how enterprise teams notice, investigate, decide, and act — and where AI can help without taking control away.

◉  Dashboard interviews＋  EAP interviews as supporting evidence

Customers use dashboards to get the numbers, then leave Zendesk to make sense of them.

Dashboards are trusted and easy to start with. Where they fall short is the step after the number: people can’t see what changed, they aren’t told when something needs attention, and they rebuild the story in spreadsheets, slides, or outside AI tools before anyone else sees it. Customers want AI that notices and explains, but they want control over who sees data and what gets done. They don’t want operational actions from a dashboard.

KEY FINDINGS

Five takeaways

1

People need help discovering changes they did not know to look for

Users 3/5 · EAP 4/7

Useful trends, outliers, and comparisons can surface before someone knows what question to ask. This is where proactive signals can reveal an issue or opportunity that routine monitoring misses.

“I feel like it's probably a gap that we're not using dashboards enough. But at the same time I don't know what I don't know.”Nick Wolf · T1
“It might catch trends that I'm not even looking at.”DJ Hartel · T3
“The main use really is to understand volume and sort of what's causing volume… certain outlier tickets. Spiking, for example…”Joseph Wood · T10
2

After spotting a signal, users need to investigate its cause

Users 5/5 · EAP 7/7

People compare, filter, drill into records, and export details to explain what changed. Manual work and external tools often fill gaps between a dashboard metric and a useful answer.

“I have to go to multiple dashboards…”Nick Wolf · T1
“The most painful thing I guess is the drill in option sometimes… it is a bit tedious as well.”Lané Lubbe · T4
“Sometimes it's really useful to drill into those, export the ticket number… and just see exactly what's happening.”Chris Boyd · T13
“We download those reports… and manually get the data within Google Sheets because we don't get like the exact report we need from analytics.”Vivi Chamorro · T12
3

Findings need to travel as clear reports, often after manual work

Users 4/5 · EAP 6/7

People turn exports into spreadsheets, BI, slides, or concise updates for leaders and clients. Operational teams may need detail; other audiences often want the takeaway and what changed.

“I use a Google spreadsheet to track… the output that they have, but I pull it from that dashboard.”Tim Santoro · T2
“I can have an Excel or a CSV table and it's enough for me. I push it into Python and I can do whatever I want.”Paula Gual · T11
“They don't ever want to see the dashboard, but they want us to give them almost like a written report or a verbal report on what is happening.”Lané Lubbe · T4
“I just want the four numbers.”Nick Wolf · T1, quoting his boss
“We don't usually use reports on a daily basis. When we use it is mostly every quarter… we need to present this to leadership.”Vivi Chamorro · T12
4

AI can help with analysis when people retain control

Users 5/5 · EAP 6/7

Participants see value in summarizing, explaining, and finding opportunities. They also need visible evidence, reliable context, appropriate permissions, and human review before consequential actions.

“I would love to have an AI tool… know the trends I'm looking for… and then have it proactively tell me when those things happen.”Nick Wolf · T1
“Having some kind of AI tool basically summarizing… what is happening as of now…”Sayd Sarshar · T7
“We've been putting all that data together, putting it in Copilot… and asking it to spot common themes and trends.”Rachel Harris · T6
“If AI suggested an action… it needs to be approved by someone before the action gets taken.”Lané Lubbe · T4
5

People want AI to make dashboard creation easier

Users 4/5 · EAP 5/7

Participants want to describe the view they need in plain language, let AI draft it, and then refine and verify the result themselves. This could make useful dashboards easier to create and reduce dependence on a few analytics experts.

“I would love to have basically a chat conversation explaining my needs and have it build me a dashboard.”Nick Wolf · T1
“Go into analytics, have the bot pop up… and say create me a report, do these things.”DJ Hartel · T3
“This is the dashboard that I need. These are the requirements that I need for this dashboard. This is what it should look like.”Lané Lubbe · T4
“We wanted to enable everyone to go and type a query and make a report.”Chris Boyd · T13
OPPORTUNITY

Where to focus

  1. Surface changes and outliers early

    Help people notice patterns before they know what to search for.

    Users 3/5 · EAP 4/7
  2. Connect a signal to its cause

    Make comparisons, filters, and underlying records easy to reach.

    Users 5/5 · EAP 7/7
  3. Make findings easy to share

    Reduce spreadsheet work and create summaries suited to leaders and clients.

    Users 4/5 · EAP 6/7
  4. Make AI useful, explainable, and human-led

    Show evidence and context; respect permissions and require review for consequential actions.

    AI: Users 5/5 · EAP 6/7
    Trust: Users 4/5 · EAP 5/7
  5. Help people create dashboards with AI

    Start from a plain-language request, draft the view, then let people refine and verify it.

    Users 4/5 · EAP 5/7

AI GUARDRAILS
Control stays human

Participants want AI to help find and explain patterns. Sharing, access, and consequential operational actions still need a human in control.

A VISION FOR DASHBOARD BUILDER

From dashboards
to data apps.

Dashboard Builder turns metrics into focused, reusable experiences—bringing data, context, and investigation together around the decisions each team needs to make.

01↗
BUILD FOR YOUR NEEDS

What if a dashboard started with your question?

Describe the goal; let AI suggest metrics and filters; then refine and save the dashboard.

RESEARCH SIGNAL

People spend time assembling dashboards around the questions their teams need to answer.

↓
PRODUCT HYPOTHESIS

Start from the person’s goal, then generate an editable, inspectable dashboard draft.

↓
TO TEST

Can people reach a useful dashboard faster by starting with their need?

02↗
FIND PATTERNS & TRENDS

What if you could explore trends and patterns when they matter?

Compare metrics with the previous period; summarize what changed and where; ask AI for a deeper explanation when useful.

RESEARCH SIGNAL

People look for trends manually; deeper pattern analysis can add cost and noise.

↓
PRODUCT HYPOTHESIS

Show trends in view and make cross-metric pattern analysis available on request.

↓
TO TEST

When do people want deeper analysis, and what latency or cost is acceptable?

03↗
INVESTIGATE

What if drill-in felt like one continuous thought?

Keep filters as you move from a changed metric to its segments and records. AI suggests the next step; you choose whether to explore it, preview the evidence, and save or share your path.

RESEARCH SIGNAL

Drill-in often stops before the useful detail, sending people to exports or other tools.

↓
PRODUCT HYPOTHESIS

Keep filters and time range as people move from metric to segment to record; offer optional next cuts.

↓
TO TEST

Does a continuous path reduce context switching, and which suggested cuts help?

04↗
MAKE SENSE

What if every finding could become an evidence-linked summary?

Summarize what changed and where; link claims to charts and records; review and edit before sharing.

RESEARCH SIGNAL

Findings often need to reach people who never open the dashboard.

↓
PRODUCT HYPOTHESIS

Draft editable summaries that link claims to evidence and separate facts from possible explanations.

↓
TO TEST

Do evidence-linked drafts reduce reporting effort while keeping people confident in each claim?

DESIGN PRINCIPLE

Keep the evidence close. Keep exploration in the user’s hands. Keep AI claims inspectable and editable.
