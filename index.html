✳ CUSTOMER RESEARCH · OCTOBER 2026

From insight
to action.

What happens after a dashboard raises a question? A research synthesis on how enterprise teams notice, investigate, decide, and act — and where AI can help without taking control away.

◉  Dashboard interviews＋  EAP interviews as supporting evidence

Customers use dashboards to get the numbers, then leave Zendesk to make sense of them.

Dashboards are trusted and easy to start with. Where they fall short is the step after the number: people can’t see what changed, they aren’t told when something needs attention, and they rebuild the story in spreadsheets, slides, or outside AI tools before anyone else sees it. Customers want AI that notices and explains, but they want control over who sees data and what gets done. They don’t want operational actions from a dashboard.

KEY FINDINGS

Five takeaways

1

Dashboards show metrics, not what stands out

5/5 · +1 EAP

People scan familiar dashboards to find what needs attention, but the numbers do not show whether a result is unusual or meaningful. There is no built-in baseline or week-over-week comparison; two participants rebuild comparisons elsewhere, while context such as staffing changes or excluded teams stays in people’s heads.

“For them to understand what it means and for myself to also just easily see, you know, what is at the top of the list, what needs attention.”Lané Lubbe · Dashboard interviews
2

The real deliverable is a summary, and it gets built outside Zendesk

5/5 · +1 EAP

Executives, clients, and ops colleagues often never open Zendesk. Dashboards mainly send them data, yet image exports lose data, layouts are hard to put on slides, and builders reformat everything in Sheets, Slides, Claude, or ChatGPT.

“They don’t ever want to see the dashboard but they want us to give them almost like a […] written report or a verbal report.”Lané Lubbe · Dashboard interviews

So what: Dashboards are how Zendesk data reaches people who never log in. Today that last step happens in other tools.

3

Knowing what needs attention depends on someone watching a screen

3/5

Teams run live dashboards on office TVs and second monitors. In one case, a missed SLA-breach email left an urgent ticket unpicked for 2–3 hours. Agent online status is only visible in analytics.

“You could almost set […] automations or triggers on a dashboard itself […] it almost send you a notification.”Lané Lubbe · Dashboard interviews

So what: Customers want to be told, not to watch. Alerts must stay quiet and specific, or people learn to ignore them.

4

AI demand is real, and it comes with conditions

5/5 · +1 EAP

People want AI that spots changes on its own, explains them, and drafts summaries. They do not want AI deciding who receives data, granting access, or acting without approval. Two participants already put Zendesk data into outside AI tools. One stopped reading generic AI recommendations after a few irrelevant ones.

“They’re not going to blame the bot. They’re going to blame me because I own it.”DJ Hartel · Dashboard interviews

So what: If we don’t explain the data inside Zendesk, customers will do it in other AI tools and use us only as a data source. Permissions, approval, and visible reasoning are adoption requirements.

5

Actions continue in connected workflows

4/5 · WORKFLOW EXCEPTION

Most operational work continues in tickets, Slack, staffing plans, and team conversations. The prioritization exercise drew no picks for “carry out a response” or “check whether it worked.” Pedro’s connected sales and QA workflows qualify this finding: systems can move work forward, while a person approves consequential decisions.

“Nothing happens quickly enough that I’m only alerted to it from a dashboard.”Nick Wolf · Dashboard interviews

So what: Don’t invest in in-dashboard actions now. Support the weekly and monthly decisions dashboards already inform.

OPPORTUNITY

Where to focus

  1. Show change against a baseline

    So readers can tell good, bad, or normal at a glance.

    5/5 · +1 EAP
  2. Turn a dashboard into an accurate summary

    For people who never open Zendesk.

    5/5 · +1 EAP
  3. Alert owners to the few changes that matter

    Without noise.

    3/5
  4. Make every number explainable

    Definitions, data source, and freshness.

    4/5 · +1 EAP
  5. Build in AI guardrails

    Respect permissions, require approval, and show reasoning.

    5/5 · +1 EAP

EXPECTATIONS

Where customers see AI opportunity

01
CREATE WITH A CONVERSATION5/5 · +1 EAP

Build dashboards and reports from a plain-language request

Start with the user’s goal, generate the view, then let them refine the data, layout, and metrics.

“I would love to have basically a chat conversation explaining my needs and have it build me a dashboard.”Nick Wolf · Dashboard interviews
02
NOTICE WHAT I’D MISS4/5 · +1 EAP

Surface relevant changes and explain why they matter

Highlight unusual movement, connect it to likely causes, and make alerts specific enough to earn attention.

“I would love to have an AI tool… know the trends I’m looking for… and then have it proactively tell me when those things happen.”Nick Wolf · Dashboard interviews
03
TURN SIGNALS INTO A STORY3/5 · +1 EAP

Summarize findings for people who don’t want raw data

Generate audience-ready commentary and summaries that explain what is happening, why, and what deserves follow-up. Rachel also uses Copilot today to synthesize patterns into executive-ready slides.

“It helps me see trends… ‘this is different from last week… because of this Y and Z reasons.’”Nick Wolf · Dashboard interviews
AI GUARDRAILS
Control stays human

Participants want AI to help find and explain patterns. Sharing, access, and consequential operational actions still need a human in control.

HYPOTHESES & OPPORTUNITIES

Move from dashboards to trusted data apps.

Dashboards can become a starting point for focused, reusable experiences that help teams understand a signal and carry the work forward.

01 HYPOTHESES TO TEST
Context makes insight actionable.

People will move with more confidence when a change is paired with comparisons, drivers, definitions, and supporting records.

Proactive explanations earn attention.

Teams will welcome AI that catches meaningful shifts and explains why they matter, when evidence and uncertainty are visible.

Trust depends on human control.

Review before sharing and human-led operational decisions are prerequisites for adoption.

02 OPPORTUNITIES FOR DATA APPS
Compose around a job to be done.

Let people create and refine a focused dashboard or report through plain language, then keep its logic inspectable.

Connect signal, investigation, and story.

Bring relevant metrics, comparisons, underlying data, and audience-ready summaries into one coherent experience.

Make apps governable and reusable.

Support teams in managing permissions, definitions, versions, and sharing as data experiences scale across the organization.
